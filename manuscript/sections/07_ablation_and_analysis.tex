\section{Ablation and Analysis}

\subsection{Generative sampling versus deterministic inversion}

The baseline evaluation code in the repository reports not only the best score obtained among candidate structures, but also the mean score over all candidates, the top-3 average, the thresholded success rate, the spectrum MAE relative to the target response, the binary rate of the generated patterns, and the diversity of the candidate set. This metric design already hints at the scientific question of interest. If the inverse problem were effectively single-valued, one would mostly care about one best prediction. The inclusion of diversity and candidate-set statistics signals the opposite: the practical question is whether the design procedure can populate a useful region of the feasible set.

Against that background, the expected distinction between deterministic and generative inversion becomes physically meaningful. Deterministic baselines may recover a reasonable average structure or even a reasonable average response, but they typically offer weaker candidate diversity and less opportunity for post-generation physical selection. A diffusion model, by contrast, is useful precisely because it can produce multiple binary candidates that occupy different parts of the response-conditioned design manifold. The benefit is not simply that ``more samples are better.'' The benefit is that the one-to-many character of the inverse map is preserved long enough for RCWA verification to choose among physically distinct but target-adjacent structures.

\subsection{Full response conditioning versus reduced target descriptions}

The second key ablation concerns how much of the target response is exposed to the inverse model. The present approach conditions on the full two-channel angle--wavelength tensor. A weaker alternative would be to condition on a single wavelength row, a compressed set of scalar descriptors, or a task label accompanied by a few hand-crafted parameters. Such simplifications reduce data dimensionality, but they also risk severing the connection between the input condition and the actual device physics.

The logic of the repository's task scores makes the consequences of such compression clear. Center suppression, edge behavior, and polarization leakage are all properties that can look acceptable under a reduced description and still fail after full verification. Oversimplified conditioning therefore tends to produce candidates that satisfy a local or averaged target while missing the coupled structure of the verified response. This is why the full response-space input should be regarded as more than a technical convenience. It is the minimal statement of the optical specification needed to keep the inverse problem aligned with the physics of the operator.

\subsection{Why post-generation verification changes the scientific meaning of the design loop}

The third ablation concerns reranking itself. A workflow that stops at surrogate agreement or generator confidence remains a data-driven proposal mechanism. A workflow that performs RCWA-based reranking becomes a physically closed inverse-design system. The difference is qualitative rather than incremental. Once candidate structures are compared under verified response scores, the final selection reflects the actual transfer-function behavior of the device and not just the internal confidence of the learned model.

The practical effect is especially important when candidates are close in aggregate score but differ in how they distribute error across the response field. One candidate may match the central profile yet fail in the outer-angle region; another may maintain better bandwidth support but leak into the silent polarization channel; a third may be highly binary but less faithful spectrally. Post-generation verification exposes these tradeoffs. Without it, the method would risk overclaiming physical control on the basis of a learned approximation. With it, the design loop is forced to respect the real structure of the inverse problem, including the fact that candidate ranking is itself part of the scientific content.
